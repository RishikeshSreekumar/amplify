WHAT'S IN IT

One menu bar icon.
Quite a lot behind it.

Boost is the headline, but once you're routing your Mac's sound through something, you may as well fix the rest of it too. Here's everything Amplify does.

01 / PER-APP MIXER

Every app gets its own volume knob.

macOS gives you one slider for everything. Amplify gives each app its own, from 0% (muted) up to 300%.

Turn the browser up to hear a quiet lecture. Turn Slack down so the knock-knock doesn't make you jump. Leave Music alone.

Try the sliders. The ↺ button in the real app puts an app back to 100%.

02 / EFFECTS

Seven effects. Each one is a single switch.

No plug-in chains, no knobs labelled "Q". Turn on what helps, leave the rest off.

Loudness is the one most people keep on. It makes things sound louder than plain boost with less distortion, which is exactly what you want on laptop speakers.

    03 / EQUALIZER

    A 10-band EQ, from 32 Hz to 16 kHz.

    Pick a preset or drag the bands into your own curve. Each band goes ±12 dB.

    Bass Boost does what it says. Vocal pulls voices forward. Loudness Curve is the classic smile shape that makes small speakers sound bigger at low volume.

    Presets are real. Those are the exact curves the app uses.

    FAQ

    Questions people ask

    What happens when the 7 days are up?

    The boost switches off and Amplify asks for a license key. Your settings, EQ curve and per-app levels stay where you left them, so buying picks up right where the trial ended.

    Will it damage my speakers?

    The limiter stops digital clipping, but it can't make tiny speakers bigger. Already-loud music at 300% will sound strained on a MacBook. Boost works best on things that were too quiet to begin with, like calls, lectures, podcasts and badly mixed videos. Use your ears.

    Why does it ask for "System Audio Recording" permission?

    To make your Mac's audio louder, Amplify has to read it first. That's the permission macOS uses for it. Nothing is recorded to disk and nothing is sent anywhere.

    Does it work with AirPods, headphones and external speakers?

    Yes. It boosts whatever your Mac is currently playing through, and remembers a separate level for each one. One thing to know is that while Amplify is on, all audio goes to your current default output, even from apps set to use a different one.

    Why macOS 14.2 or later?

    Amplify is built on Core Audio process taps, which Apple added in macOS 14.2. That's what lets it work without a kernel extension or a virtual audio device.

    Is there any delay?

    A few milliseconds. You won't notice it in videos or calls. Changing an app's own level also causes a short blip while Amplify rebuilds its capture.

    Can I install it with Homebrew?

    Yes. Run brew install --cask rishikeshsreekumar/tap/amplify and the trial starts the first time you open it.
